Theory

A boost converter is a non-isolated DC-DC converter that raises a lower DC voltage to a higher DC voltage by storing energy in an inductor and transferring it to the output.

Ideal CCM relation

Vout = Vin / (1 − D)

Inductor ripple

ΔIL ≈ VinD / (L fs)

Output ripple (first-order)

ΔVout ≈ IoutD / (C fs)

CCM / DCM

CCM: inductor current stays above zero. DCM: inductor current reaches zero during part of the switching period.

Losses included in this educational model

Source resistance, inductor ESR, capacitor ESR, MOSFET RDS(on), diode forward voltage/resistance and approximate switching-loss terms are included to make the results more realistic than the ideal formula alone.

Experiment Procedure

  1. Enter Vin, duty cycle, switching frequency, L, C and load.
  2. Set ESR, MOSFET, diode and switching parameters.
  3. Press Run and observe live values.
  4. Press Animate and watch the current direction change between ON and OFF states.
  5. Select each waveform and compare switching behavior.
  6. Run a parameter sweep for duty cycle, input voltage, load or component values.
  7. Add selected readings to the observation table.
  8. Export the table as CSV for your lab report.

Expected result

Increasing duty cycle increases the boost conversion ratio. Practical losses reduce the output voltage and efficiency compared with the ideal equation.

Observation Guidelines

For a typical study, keep Vin fixed and vary duty cycle. Record Vout, Iout, Pout, efficiency, inductor ripple and conduction mode. Then repeat by changing L, C, fs and Rload.
