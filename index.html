DC-DC Boost Converter

A boost converter is a non-isolated DC-DC converter that produces an output voltage higher than its input under normal boost operation.

Ideal CCM relation

Vout = Vin / (1 − D)

Here D is the duty ratio, defined as Ton/T.

Switch ON

The MOSFET conducts. The inductor stores energy from the source while the diode is reverse biased. The capacitor supports the load.

Switch OFF

The MOSFET turns off. The inductor releases stored energy through the diode into the capacitor and load, raising the output voltage.

Key equations

  • T = 1/fs
  • Ton = D·T
  • Toff = (1−D)·T
  • ΔIL ≈ Vin·D/(L·fs)
  • Iout = Vout/R
  • Pout = Vout·Iout
  • η = Pout/Pin × 100%

CCM / DCM

In continuous conduction mode (CCM), inductor current remains above zero during the complete switching period. If it reaches zero, the converter enters discontinuous conduction mode (DCM).

Why non-ideal parameters matter

Real converters have MOSFET conduction loss, diode forward drop, inductor resistance, capacitor ESR and switching losses. These reduce the output voltage and efficiency compared with an ideal model.

Experiment Procedure

  1. Enter the input voltage and converter component values.
  2. Choose duty cycle and switching frequency.
  3. Press Run Simulation.
  4. Observe the animated switching circuit.
  5. Select different waveforms and study their shapes.
  6. Change duty cycle and compare theoretical and simulated output.
  7. Use Parameter Sweep for systematic analysis.
  8. Click Add Reading to build the observation table.
  9. Export the table as CSV if required.

Expected learning outcomes

  • Understand energy transfer in a boost converter.
  • Relate duty cycle to output voltage.
  • Understand switching waveforms and inductor ripple.
  • Observe the effect of non-ideal losses.
  • Distinguish CCM and DCM operation.
